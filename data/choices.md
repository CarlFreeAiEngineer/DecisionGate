# Multiple-choice decisions (choices-v1)

`choices.jsonl` is a small original synthetic dataset for a new decision call, `choose(content, question, options)`, which returns the index of the option that best answers the question about the content. Each line is one JSON record with the fields `schema_version` (1), `id` (a string like `choices-v1-047`), `group_id` (shared by records that come from the same content passage, always within one split), `content` (1 to 4 sentences of English or, for the language-identification family, a short passage in another language), `question`, `options` (a list of 2 to 6 short strings, not always in the same order), `label` (the 0-based index into `options` that answers the question), `label_type` (`choice`), `task_family` (a snake_case name such as `support_routing` or `sentiment`), `source` (`decisiongator-original-choices-v1`), `provenance` (a fixed object recording that the content, question, options, and label were individually authored, with no model calls or template expansion), `license` (`CC0-1.0`), `rationale` (one sentence explaining the label), `review_status`, and `split`. Only `content`, `question`, and `options` are meant as model inputs; the other fields are metadata and must not be used as features. Every record has `review_status: synthetic_unreviewed`: the author checked label consistency while writing the data, but nothing has had independent human review, and the dataset should be treated accordingly. The authors intend all records in this file, and the source material behind them, to be available under CC0-1.0 (public domain dedication), to the extent rights in generated material can be dedicated; see <https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode>.

Most content passages appear twice, under two different questions with two different correct options, sharing one `group_id` and one `split`; this makes the question necessary, since reading the content alone cannot answer both. Some passages mention two plausible options where only one is actually correct, and some questions use negation ("which team was ruled out", "which day was it before it changed") to test that the model is not just pattern-matching on keywords.

Training, validation, and calibration cover seven families, spread evenly: `support_routing` (billing, technical support, sales, account access), `request_type` (refund, replacement, cancellation, appointment, information), `sentiment` (positive, negative, mixed, neutral), `urgency_level` (low, medium, high, emergency), `language_of_text` (identifying which language a passage, or part of it, is written in), `day_or_time_lookup` (picking the day, month, or time actually stated among plausible candidates), and `unit_or_quantity` (picking the number-with-units actually stated among plausible candidates). Test adds two families that appear nowhere else in the file, `document_type` (invoice, receipt, contract, resume, recipe, news article) and `animal_or_object` (short descriptions naming an animal or an object), plus 6 records using new passages from the training families. No content passage or `group_id` is shared across splits.
